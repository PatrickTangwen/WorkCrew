The trustees submit their annual report and the financial statemeots for the year ended 28 February
2017.
Structure, governance and management
The Charity became a Charitable Incorporated Organisation on 9 March 2015. The Charity became
registered with the Charity Commission on this date, number 1160799.
Summary of the objects of the Charity
The objects of the charity are the relief of people affected by dementia by:
1) Supporting them in achieving recognition of their specific needs;
2) Advising their families in order to find appropriate services for them;
3) Ensuring statutory services and other relevant organisations are aware of their needs and the
needs of their carers; and
4) advancing the education of the general public in all areas relating to dementia
Summary of the main activities undertaken for the public benefit
Our charity provides help to dementia sufferers which is not available through the usual channels. We
raise funds to improve the environments in nursing homes by arranging events and providing
dementia friendly equipment to enable residents to take part in s variety of activities. It is vital for
residents to receive adequate stimulation and remain engaged with those around them as long as
possible.
Summary of the main achievements during the period
This year has seen us involved in several large projects and in finalising work on projects started last year.
1) The project to provide an interesting and stimulating environment for residents whilst walking along
the large length of corridors within the home has been completed. CQC commented on the value
of the interactive displays on the corridors.
2) Following the success of the boards on the corridors we were asked to provide similar interactive
boards for the cottages. These were amazingly time consuming to assemble but have proved very
useful to residents.
3) This year has seen the introduction of freshly cooked food within the home, a change from the pre-
prepared food which was available previously. This change was initiated by the service providers
and we were asked to serve on the steering group for the change. Our role was to liaise with
relatives and ensure the changes being made met the needs and requirements of service users.
The initiative has been a great success
4) A major project this year has been our "GROW" project. This involved the provision of safe and
secure outside spaces for the use of residents on each cottage within the home because research
shows that access to outdoor spaces greatly enhances the well being of those suffering from
dementia.
We have been successful in a bid to the Awards for All — Big Lottery Fund to help fund a
substantial part of this project. We have worked with Sheffield University on the design for the
gardens. The outside spaces have not yet been completed and this project will run into the next
financial year.

Summary of the main achievements during the period - continued
5) Our annual garden party continues to grow in popularity. This is an event which is greatly valued
by relatives as it provides them with a day for the whole family to be together in as normal a setting
as possible. People from the community also join us for a family afternoon out. Attendance grows
year on year and this year was the biggest so far. Attractions included a marching band, a visit
from a small animal zoo, an entertainer, and donkeys from a donkey sanctuary as well as the
usual attractions of a BBQ, cake stall, cream tees, tombola, raffle and much more. This is a
massive undertaking which takes a great deal of time to organise and deliver but which is such a
valued and rewarding undertaking.
6) At the request of several of our relatives we provided small blankets for each of our residents for
use when sitting still or out and about in wheelchairs to keep them cosy. There were some
blankets on each cottage, but sometimes there weren't enough to go round, so, working with staff
and managers we provided one for each resident.
7) We are now engaged with the Art House in Sheffield and are funding their art class for people with
enduring mental illness to make us 30 tactile boards for use with our residents.
8) In November we were asked to help with a campaign to save Hurlfield View day care and respite
centre. The facility was due to be closed by Sheffield City Council in March. We provided help and
support to relatives and carers who were fighting the closure. Sadly the facility closed, which is a
great loss to Sheffield.
9) In January Birch Avenue nursing home was threatened with privatisation and we have been
working with relatives to try and find a suitable solution to the problems currently being
encountered.
10) Helping relatives understand all the issues which face them when they reach the point where they
have to put a loved one into full time care continues to take time. Helping relatives to understand
continuing healthcare funding, deprivation of liberty assessments and much more and signpost
them to those who can help them is an ongoing task.
11) Christmas was a busy time in the home with a great deal of activity for residents. It was difficult to
find any time in December for a get together but relatives/csrers met for an update on what was
happening in and around the home and for a little festive cheer.
Plans for future periods
The coming year will see us finalising several of our ongoing projects, such as the provision if safe outdoor
spaces and the creation of tactile boards for use by residents in the horne. The garden party will no doubt
take time and effort once again and we will continue to offer support to relatives with any problems they
might encounter as they navigate the NHS in order to obtain good quality care for their loved ones.
Reserves poiicy
The charity has free reserves of F8, 534 at the end of the year. The charity aims to hold sufficient
reserves to pay outstanding creditors. The charity will use some current free reserves to help fund
the GROW project.
