Objectives and activities
Summary of the objects of the charity set
out in its governing document
The purposes of the Methodist Church are and shall be
deemed to have been since the Date of Union the
advancement of:
a) The Christian faith in accordance with the doctrinal
standards and discipline of The Methodist Church;
b) Any charitable purpose for the time being of any
Connexional, District, Circuit, local or other
organization ofThe Methodist Church;
c) Any charitable purpose for the time being of any
society or institution subsidiary or ancillary to The
Methodist Church;
d) Any purpose for the time being of any charity being
a charity subsidiary or ancillary to The Methodist
Church.
Summary of the main activities
undertaken for the public benefit in
relation to these objects (include within

d) Any purpose for the time being of any charity being

The Circuit produces a quarterly preaching plan to ensure
regular acts of worship take place at each of the sixteen
churches in the Circuit. These are open to all people without
charge and include regular provision of the Christian
Sacraments.
All six ministers are deeply involved in community work
including, community cohesion, churches together
organisations. One acts as a Trustee of the Queens Hall
Action on Poverty charity which also receives practical
support from members of the churches.
The Circuit considers the safety of children and vulnerable
adults to be of the highest priority and has Safeguarding
Compliance Policies and procedures which are known about
and acted on in the churches and at circuit events. The
Circuit trains over 250 members in Safeguarding matters.
Ministers are involved in schools, including leading
assemblies and being governors/ chaplains. Two schools are
Anglican/Methodist Primary Schools.
Ministers are involved in 'End of Life' matters including
pastoral care to the bereaved and taking of funerals for both
members of the church and members of the general public.
We employ a part-time Circuit Administrator and a part time
pastoral worker.
We have two part time Workers who are employed by the
Circuit working with Young People and with Families and
Schools respectively.
We confirm the trustees have had regard to the Charity
Commission's guidance on public benefit.

Section 0
Achievements and performance
Summary of the main achievements of
the charity during the year
The Circuit successfully ran a number of discipleship courses
during the year.
Trinity Methodist Church continued its Messy Church venture
supported by the circuit family worker with financial support
from the circuit and district.
The circuit was represented in the Churches Together in
Wigan Movement and took part in a Party in the Park.
The Circuit Stewards held a Prayer, Praise & Plod canalside
walk open to all, then a picnic.
Over 100 people participated in Safeguarding courses.
The Local Preachers held a Continuing Study Day.
Tontine Methodist Church although not on the model deed,
officially ceased to meet on the 4th December 2016
The private trustees are currently dealing with sale of the
church in accordance with the original deed.

Financial Review
I
Brief statement of the charity's policy on
reserves
Details of any funds materially in deficit
The Reserves Policy for the Wigan Circuit is to hold a sum
equivalent to six months' average expenditure. This is
deemed to be sufficient to meet any unforeseen item of
major expenditure on manses and / or to be able to
continue, in the short term, funding planned activities in the
event of the closure of a large church or an inability to raise
the full Circuit Assessment from the churches. As part of our
forward planning, expenditure forecasts are prepared
looking three years ahead.
It is necessary to hold sufficient working capital to enable the
quarterly payment of stipends and business expenses for five
ministers, two youth workers, a part time pastoral worker
and a part time circuit administrator and our contribution to
the Bolton & Rochdale District.
