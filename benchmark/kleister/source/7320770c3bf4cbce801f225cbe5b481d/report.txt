St. Augustine's Church is situated in the town of Hedon in the East Riding of Yorkshire. lt is part of the
Diocese of York within the Church of England.
Objectives and public beneN
The promotion of the Gospel of Our Lord Jesus Christ according to the doctrines and practices of the Church
of England.
The PCC aim to promote the whole mission of the Church, pastoral, evangelistic, social and ecumenical
within the Padish.
When planning our activities for the year, the incumbent and the PCC have considered the Commission's
guidance on public benefit and, especially, the specific guidance on charities for the advancement of religion
ln particular, we try to enable ordinary people to five out their faith as part of our parish community and to
facilitate this it is important that we maintain the fabric of St. Augustine's Church.

To witness the love of God through worship, fellowship and service.

PCC Committee
The Criminal Records Bureau checks have been carried out for Trustees where necessary.
Eight planned and two extra-ordinary meetings took place during 2015. Average attendance was 12
Trustees.
Issues relating to Services, Finance, Building Restoration and Development, Special Events, Diocesan and
Deanery Synod and all other matters necessary for the day to day running of the Church were discussed and
dealt with at PCC meetmgs.
The Standing Committee comprising Vicar, Churchwardens, Secretary, Treasurer and Lay Vice-Chair met
regularly to deal with urgent issues and prioritise issues for full PCC meetings.
An application for grant aid for the next phase of restoration wes made to the Listed Places of Worship. The
Architect Matthew Thomas was engaged to help the Secretary Dorothy Winter complete the application.
Unfortunately this application was unsuccessful but encouragement given to re-apply.
During the year the pCC decided the next Development project - the erection of a glass inner porch to the
main West door of the Church. This would retain the heat and enable the outer doors to be opened wide
making the entrance more welcoming. The existing screening has deteriorated and the doors themselves
difficult to open. It has been necessary for the North door of the Church to be used as the main entrance
other than for special services. The project would involve removing the existing threshold providing better
access for the disabled and refurbishment of the doors. Money from bequests has been received, grants will
be applied for and fundraising events planned to raise the moriey to pay for this exciting development.
Eight Church members remain licenced by the Bishop of Hull to assist with the serving of Communion—
Wendy Bond, Carol Gronous, Dorothy Winter, Mary Johnson, Gordon Ellerby, Sue Swales, Catherine Dunn
and Virginia I-lail. This authorisation will need to be reviewed September 2017.
Three Church members iicenced by the Archbishop are now trained as Recognised Parish Assistants:-
Dorothy Winter in leading worship, Sue Swales and Jean Perkins in working with children.
Finance
The accounts enclosed with this report are self-explanatory but the following notes help to explain the result.
General income included in unrestricted funds of 635,640 was up by 8% compared to 2014 when it was
932,837. This increase is mainly due to increase in Special Service Collections, Event Income and Other
Income, Regular Giving through the Free Will Envelope Offering Scheme and the Collection Plate was
similar to 2014. General Costs of 833,946 were up by 2% compared to the 2014 figure of 532, 026. This
masks, however, a welcome increase in Parish Giving to the Diocese of E3,650 to 912,500. This was made
possible by the payment of a previous large gas liability arising in 2013 and a change in gas supplier. Light
and Heat costs decreased in 2015 by 46% to 83,890. Tax reclaimed under the Gift aid scheme was E3,798
which showed an increase of 14% over 2014 with the help of a relaxation of the rules by the Inland Revenue.
The Restoration and Development Funds continue to receive income with donations to all funds totalling
59,254 and other income bemg 82,417. There has been no major expenditure ln the year.
The new incumbent Jon Wright has been in office for his first full financial year and it is hoped his good work
will lead to a further increase in net income to enable more money to be spent on mission and to provide
sufficient funds to maintain and iinprove the actual Saint Augustine's building.

Accounting policies
The financial statements have been prepared in accordance with the Church Accounting Regulations
2006 together with Accounting and Reporting by Charities: Statement of Recommended Practice,
Accounting and Reporting by Charities.
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention using the receipts
and payments basis. The financial statements include all transactions, assets and liabilities for
which the PCC is responsible in law. They do not include the accounts of church groups that owe
their affiliation to another body or those that are informal gathedings of church members.
a)
Grants and donations
A donation or grant to the Church is recognised as incoming funds when all the following
conditions have been met:
i)
the Church is legally entitled to receipt;
ii)
there is reasonable assurance of receipt; and
iii)
its monetary value can be measured with sufficient reliability.
b)
Fund accounting
incoming resources that may be applied for the Church's general purposes are treated as
'unrestdicted' incoming resources and are credited to the 'unrestricted funds'.
Funding for
purposes designated for a specific purpose by the Trustees is credited to 'designated funds'.
Where a donation or grant is required to be used for a specific purpose, the amount
concerned is treated as a 'restricted' incoming resource and is credited to the appropriate
'restricted fund'.
