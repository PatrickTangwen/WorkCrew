Governance and management
The charity is a company limited by guarantee and registered charity. It is operated under
the rules of its memorandum and articles of association dated 18th July 2007. It has no
share capital and the liability of each member in the event of winding-up is limited to £1.
New directors are elected at the AGM and by co-opting at other times.
Aims and objectives
The protection and preservation of spiritual, emotional and psychological health by the
provision of counselling and supportive help to mainly women, but not exclusively so, in
need thereof.
Summary of the main activities undertaken for the public benefit
1. The counselling service provides affordable, accessible counselling support for
people in need thereof;
2. The service responds quickly to people in crisis, minimising the risk of escalation of
distress and mental health problems;
3. The service targets the most vulnerable, isolated and disadvantaged people,
addressing a need in an area of most distress;
4. The service addresses issues, including, domestic abuse, sexual violence and
harassment, crime, addiction and mental health problems.

The Nottingham Women’s Counselling Service
Summary of the main achievements during the period
The Service continues to attract counsellors in advanced training as placements. These
students are supported by a placement package that ensures that their practice is ethical,
professional and safe and this enables us to provide a very low cost service for people
who may not be eligible for counselling provision elsewhere.
The service supports twenty five counsellors on placement at any one given time in
addition to four clinical supervisors and trainers to provide in house professional support.
There are two part time employed staff covering the administration and service delivery.
Over three hundred and fifty new clients were seen during the past year with over four
thousand hours booked for face to face counselling.
The clinical supervision course continues to be very popular attracting people from all
parts of the country and is also a valuable source of income.
The continuing professional development courses have included, safeguarding, domestic
violence awareness, working with suicidal clients, counselling for depression and
sexuality.
One of the most significant developments has been the contract we have with an HR
company providing counselling for City Care employees, this development has enabled
the service to continue to survive in, which is still, a very difficult market.
A quote from the HR company, Cope Ergonomics
“We have always found that Counselling Xtra (NWCS) provides a reliable and
quality service and have received excellent feed back from our service users”.
Working in partnership with the University of Nottingham and the Association for Person
Centred therapies will enable the service to produce valuable data through a new
outcomes monitoring program. This data will be used to map the performance of the
service and the wellbeing of the clients in line with statutory services and will be a useful
tool in encouraging other fund providers to support the Service and the valuable work it
provides for the community
The Service works closely with other community and statutory agencies to provide
counselling for their clients. These agencies include, The Family Intervention Project.
This is a multi agency forum dealing with the topmost antisocial families in Nottingham.
Women’s Aid Integrated service, provide support and professional advice for victims of
domestic violence and their families.
Referrals are also received from General practitioners, crisis teams, the Police, and many
other support services in Nottingham.
We at NWCS continue to follow the aims and objectives of the Charity, being mindful of
the reasons for our continued existence as a Company, passionately believing in the
Person Centred Approach to therapy and the positive effect it has on people’s lives.
Main risks
Identifying funding and deploying staff resources to the maximum effect are given priority.
NWCS negotiated a substantial reduction in the rent of Warren House for 2014/15 and
this was a short term lease arrangement which was to end in March 2015. Fortunately,
this agreement has been extended for the next three years with options after eighteen
months for Family Care to review the rent agreement.
