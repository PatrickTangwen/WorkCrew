The main source of income in 2016, as in prior years, has been the government early years funding
(73%). Parent fees for additional sessions (additional to the 15 hours covered by funding) make up a
large proportion of the remaining income (22%). Parent fees have decreased from 2015 when they
made up 28% of the total income. This is somewhat to do with the timing of invoicing and the fact
that we use cash accounting rather than the accruals basis; for the last 2 years we have sent out the
invoices for the Spring term in December. Due to the fact we use cash accouting, we recognise any
cash received before 31st December in the Parents fees for that year. In 2015 we had only £3.7k left
unpaid, whereas this year £7.6k remained unpaid. This means we were able to recognise £3.9k
more in 2015 compared to 2016, simply due to the timing of payments. The remaining drop in
parents fees is due to a slight reduction in sessions filled in 2016 compared to the previous year, and
we also received slightly more in Early Years funding in 2016 (children funded via this rather than
parents fees).
Fundraising income is healthy at £4.3k, remaining fairly consistent year on year to the dedicated
efforts of our fundraising team. Fundraising remains an important means of generating income for
Acorns and is raised through events and parent donations. A restricted reserve is not required as
the money has never been raised with a particular use specified to donors. Other income includes
items such as bank interest and sale of uniform.
Salaries, PAYE & NI have increased 9% compared to 2015 by £8.4k. The main factor in this is due to
a period of 3 months in which we were paying salaries for 2 Managers. This was to ensure that a
handover between our existing and new Manager could take place. They worked together from the
beginning of June but were both paid for 3 months simultaneously due to the summer holidays. Our
administrator also increased her hours from 10 to 15 per week as of May 2016.
The cleaning expenses have shot up by 84% compared to last year, an increase of £3.2k. This is due
to some overlap between cleaning companies working as we were having problems with the
efficiency and standards of one and so brought in another to take over. Hopefully we will see these
costs reduce again in 2017.
The spend on resources and equipment has decreased by 40% compared to 2015. Resources
continue to be replaced as and when required; it was noted that a particularly large amount was
spent in 2015 - it had approximately doubled from the £6.6k spent in 2014 (this was largely due to
the timing of purchases).
Overall a profit of £5,2k has been made in 2016. For further information, a cash flow statement is
also provided which shows the overall increase in cash over 2016 was £5.2k. This is a good result,
and ways in which the cash will be spent to further enhance the setting will be discussed below.

From Jan 2013 it was decided that equipment should no longer be capitalised and depreciated over
its useful life, but to be expensed fully in the year of purchase. Assets which have already been
capitalised will continue to be depreciated, straight line, over their useful life from the month of
purchase. Therefore the reduction in the fixed asset NBV simply equates to the depreciation charge
for the year.
Acorn Under Fives have a healthy cash balance - £172k at the end of 2016. Whenever possible, and
when required, cash is reinvested into the preschool to improve facilities and the learning
environment for children. We have a large redevelopment project planned which we hope to
undertake in the near future; this would refurbish and extend the existing building and is anticipated
to cost in the region of £150k. Money is therefore being set aside to prepare for this, in addition to
having to cover redundancy costs (approximately £20k), to ensure that the liabilities could be met in
a worst case scenario. It can therefore be justified that this money is not able to be reinvested at
the current time on charitable activity.
