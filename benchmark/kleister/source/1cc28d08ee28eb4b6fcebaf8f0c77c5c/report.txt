The Governors present their report together with financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2015.
This report is prepared in accordance with the Scheme approved by the Charity Commissioners and the
recommendation of the Statement of Recommended Practice "Accounting and Reporting by Charities" issued
in March 2005 and complies with applicable law.
ACHIEVEMENT AND PERFORMANCE
Review of developments and achievements
The Trustees during the past year accomplished the conversion of the upper floors of 8 Bridge Street and 5
Gold Street into residential flats. This work was accomplished considerably under the cost estimates, and the
flats are fully occupied under leasehold agreements. The Trustees are heartened by this development as
these upper floors were proving to be increasingly difficult to let as business premises. The success of this
venture has encouraged the Trustees to make plans for a similar conversion of 1A and 3 Gold Street. They
hope to achieve this conversion during the forthcoming year.
The Trustees have kept a careful eye on the receipt of rents from its business properties. We continue to
have several tenants who are in arrears and we have monitored their situations, taking the view that in the
short run having premises occupied is better than incurring the costs of vacancy. We have, nonetheless,
instructed our advisers, Messrs Budworth Hardcastle, to take all available actions to maximise the timely
receipt of rents.
During the past year it has been drawn to our attention that our licence arrangements with the Lowdown, which
occupies the Old School Building, Kingswell Street, have not been reviewed for many years and that a number
of problems have arisen that required being addressed. We have negotiated a new lease with the Lowdown,
including a peppercorn rent, to replace the former licence. We have agreed with them on a course of
remediation of building faults, some of which are to be done by the Lowdown at their expense and some of
which have been paid for by the Trustees. We are confident that these new arrangements will serve both
parties more satisfactorily for the foreseeable future.
Our investment portfolio, managed by Messrs Brewin Dolphin, has continued to perform well despite skids in
the stock market. We consider that we have been fortunate to have converted some £400,000 into cash to
fund the conversion of business to residential properties; had this money remained invested in equities we
believe we might well have suffered a loss due to the market's volatility. We continue to monitor performance
of the portfolio and are in regular conversation with our investment advisers. We believe that we have a good
working relationship with them which we hope can continue.
The availability of cash for the grant-making work of the Foundation continues to be the paramount concern of
the Trustees, and the volume of applications for grants shows no sign of slackening. The Trustees are
pleased at the quality of the applications being received, and consider it vitally important that we continue to aid
as many young people as we can with their educational pursuits.
The Schools Committee continue their regular visits to Church of England schools and report their findings to
the full Board meetings. In particular, our relationship with All Saints School has continued to deepen during
the past year. We are pleased to have been able to make a major grant towards their work of improving
literacy. Members of the Trustees continue to serve as Governors of the School and the Trustees receive
regular and detailed reports. We continue to keep a watchful eye on the progress of this school.
The Trustees have reviewed arrangements with their various advisers during the past year, and have entered
into Terms of Engagement with our legal advisers, Messrs. Hewitsons, LLP.
The Trustees continue to take seriously their responsibilities for this distinguished Foundation and believe that
our actions over the past year have been commensurate with the objective of continuing its work into the
future.

FINANCIAL REVIEW
Reserves policy
It is the policy of the charity to hold the property and investments portfolios to generate (taking one year with
another) sufficient income to continue current levels of grant making in real terms. A balance is held in liquid
funds to cover management expenditure as and when it arises.
At the 31 December 2015, the amount of the liquid reserves in the unrestricted fund amounted to £179,202
(2014: £26,690) which the Governors are satisfied will cover a year's management expenditure.
Financial review
The results for the year are set out on page 7.
No further sums are to be invested in the property reserve fund in which £255,000 has been invested. At 31
December 2015 the value of the investments was £411,468 (2014: £422,097) excluding cash at the brokers of
£167,767 (2014: £415,621).
The general fund amounted to £2,729,898 at 31 December 2015 (2014: £2,725,042). The increase during
the year arose due to net incoming resources of £12,889 and investment revaluation of £(8,033).
STRUCTURE, GOVERNANCE AND MANAGEMENT
Objects, organisation, policies and changes
The Charity is governed by a Scheme dated 27 November 1986, as amended 5 October 2004, 27 September
2005, and 6 July 2011, framed under the Endowed Schools Acts (the 1986 Scheme) which has reference to all
earlier schemes, as subsequently amended.
The body of Trustees consists of one ex-officio Governor, three nominative Governors and eight Co-optative
Governors, as listed on page 1 of this report.
The terms of appointment, as laid out in the Charity's scheme, are as follows:
Ex officio Governors - two appointed by Northamptonshire County Council, as local education authority and
one by the Peterborough Diocesan Board of Education. Those appointed by the Northamptonshire County
Council hold office for a term ending on the date of appointment of their successors, whilst those appointed by
the Peterborough Diocesan Board of Education are appointed for a term of four years.
Co-optative Governors - appointed for a term of five years by resolution of the Governors passed at a special
meeting.

STRUCTURE, GOVERNANCE AND MANAGEMENT
Objects, organisation, policies and changes
The Governors confirm that they have referred to the guidance contained in the Charity Commission's general

have attended that School for not less than two years or who are resident in the Borough and subject

Borough of Northampton. In addition, the Governors are able to award grants for further education
and university courses beyond the Borough of Northampton where appropriate for locally resident
students, subject to the full provisions of the 1986 scheme.

1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Basis of preparing the financial statements
The financial statements of the charity have been prepared in accordance with the Charities SORP
(FRSSE) 'Accounting and Reporting by Charities: Statement of Recommended Practice applicable to
charities preparing their accounts in accordance with the Financial Reporting Standard for Smaller
Entities (the FRSSE) (effective 1 January 2015)", the Financial Reporting Standard for Smaller Entities
(effective January 2015) and the Charities Act 2011. The financial statements have been prepared
under the historical cost convention with the exception of investments which are included at market
value, as modified by the revaluation of certain assets.
Income
All income is recognised in the Statement of Financial Activities once the charity has entitlement to the
funds, it is probable that the income will be received and the amount can be measured reliably.
Expenditure
Liabilities are recognised as expenditure as soon as there is a legal or constructive obligation
committing the charity to that expenditure, it is probable that a transfer of economic benefits will be
required in settlement and the amount of the obligation can be measured reliably. Expenditure is
accounted for on an accruals basis and has been classified under headings that aggregate all cost
related to the category. Where costs cannot be directly attributed to particular headings they have been
allocated to activities on a basis consistent with the use of resources.
Grants are charged in the financial statements when paid. Some grants are awarded for disbursement
of the funds in a subsequent accounting period.
Raising funds
Raising funds includes all expenditure incurred by the charity to raise funds for its charitable purposes
and includes costs of all fundraising activities, events and non-charitable trading.
Charitable expenditure
Costs of charitable activities are incurred on the charity's educational operations, including support
costs and costs relating to the governance of the charity apportioned to charitable activities.
Governance costs
Governance costs represent the costs of the Clerk's time attributable to Governors' meetings and the
cost of accountancy services.
Tangible fixed assets
Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off each asset over its estimated
useful life.
Freehold property - 2% on cost
The transitional provisions of Financial Reporting Standard Number 15 have been adopted in respect of
the carrying value of freehold property. One property was last revalued in 1996 and this valuation has
been adopted as the book amount to be depreciated and hence no revaluations are to be made in the
foreseeable future unless required under Financial Reporting Standard Number 11.
Assets costing more than £1,000 are capitalised.
Investments
Investment property is shown at most recent valuation. Any aggregate surplus or deficit arising from
changes in market value is transferred to a revaluation reserve.
Taxation
The charity is exempt from tax on its charitable activities.
