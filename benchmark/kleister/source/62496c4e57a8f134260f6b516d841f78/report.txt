Naree Shakti – Women’s Strength
Report of the Trustees
For the year ended 31 March 2016
Naree Shakti’s Trustees are pleased to present their annual report and independently
examined financial statements for the year ended 31st March 2016. The Trustees have
adopted the provisions of the Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP 2005)
(Accounting Standards Charities Act 2011).
INTRODUCTION
Naree Shakti is a registered charity, registered from 16th
May 2006. It was set up by Asian
women to provide services and activities to support and empower Asian women in the
London Borough of Enfield. Through this work Naree Shakti helps and encourages women
who are isolated, discouraged or depressed to overcome difficult periods in their lives and to
take positive steps towards improving their own health and well-being, and to discover new
opportunities for participating in and benefiting from the community.
STRUCTURE, GOVERNANCE AND MANAGEMENT
The Charity is managed by a board of trustees which is ultimately responsible for all the
aspects of the overall management of the organisation. The trustees meet on a regular basis
to conduct the business of the organisation and review progress.
The trustees are elected annually at the Annual General Meeting of the organisation.
The Constitution stipulates that the board of trustees will comprise of a Chair, Secretary,
Treasurer, Vice-Chair, Joint Secretary and Joint-Treasurer (the Office Bearers).
The Chair of the trustees is responsible for the induction of any new trustee which involves
awareness of the trustee's responsibilities, the governing document, administrative
procedures, the history and philosophical approach of the charity.
The trustees review the risks that the charity faces on a quarterly basis. This is to ensure
that grants received from various funding agencies are utilised for the purpose that they
have been granted. To ensure minimal risks take place, that may have an effect on the aims
and objectives of the organisation and the expectations of funders, these risks are covered
by our insurance policy and are also monitored by way of staff reports, which highlight the
planned activities. The trustees recognise there are some areas of work which require the
acceptance and management of risk if our key objectives are to be achieved.
OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
Naree Shakti is a non-sectarian organisation set up by the Asian Women to provide services
and activities to support and empower Asian Women and their families in the London
Borough of Enfield.
The charity's object and its principal activity are to promote and deliver health and social
care services to Asian communities. The key objectives of the organisation are:

PUBLIC BENEFIT STATEMENT
Naree Shakti, without distinction of ethnic, cultural, religious or political difference provides
public benefit to relieve social isolation of South East Asian women in Enfield by providing
opportunities to meet with other women and enable them participate fully in social, cultural
and civic activities in their communities. It also provides public benefit to relieve poverty by
providing advice, information, advocacy and support to South Asian Women and advance
their education and access to opportunities otherwise not available due to language and
cultural barriers. The organisation provides health related activities to reduce health
inequalities within the South Asian community in Enfield and various recreational activities to
improve quality of life.

Naree Shakti has achieved its aims and objectives this year. Key services achieved their

Future plans
In 2016-17 we plan to continue our health and social support activities. We are also actively

Reserves policy
The Board has assessed the charity’s requirements for reserves in the light of the main risks
to the organisation. As a result, the Board has approved a policy whereby the unrestricted
funds not committed, should be held in reserve and maintained at a level which ensures that
Naree Shakti’s core activity could continue during a period of unforeseen difficulty. The
target reserve amount represents at least 6 months’ (26 weeks) expenditure and will be
reviewed annually.
Trustees and their responsibilities
Charity trustees are the people who serve on the governing body of a charity. They may be
known as trustees, directors, board members, governors or committee members. The
principles and main duties are the same in all cases.
Trustees have, and must accept, ultimate responsibility for directing the affairs of a charity,
and ensuring that it is solvent, well-run, and meeting the needs for which it has been set up.

1. Accounting basis.
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, modified
to include the revaluation of certain fixed assets, and in accordance with the Financial
Reporting Standard for Smaller Entities (effective January 2008).
The statements have also been prepared in accordance with the Statement of
Recommended Practice (SORP) - Accounting and Reporting by Charities SORP 2005
issued in March 2005.
Fixed Assets
All Fixed assets are initially recorded at cost.
Depreciation
Depreciation is calculated so as to write off the cost of an asset, less its estimated residual
value, over the useful economic life of that asset as follows:
25% per annum on a reducing balance.
2. Cash Flow Statement
The trustees have taken advantage of the exemption in Financial Reporting Standard No 1
(revised) from including a cash flow statement in the financial statements on the grounds
that the charity is small.
3. Income
Income is recognised in the period to which it relates, unless specified otherwise by the
funder. Project funding is, in general, repayable if not expended within the relevant project.
Such income is only recognised to the extent that it ceases to be repayable. The income is
accounted for on a receivable basis.
4. Resource Expended
Resources expended are included in the Statement of Financial Activities inclusive of VAT
which cannot be recovered. Direct charitable expenditure include includes the direct costs of
the activities. Where such costs relate to more than one functional cost category, they have
been apportioned in line with the direct costs of the relevant service.
5. Taxation
Naree Shakti is a registered charity and is not liable for corporation tax on its income under
section 505 of the Income and Corporation Taxes Act 1988 to the extent that it is applied to
its charitable activities.
6. Status
Naree Shakti is a registered charity.

MISSION STATEMENT
Naree Shakti which actually means ‘Women’s Strength’ is a non sectarian
organisation which set up by Asian women to provide services and activities
to support and empower Asian Women in Enfield.
It aims without distinction of ethnic, cultural, religious, or political difference
to provide services which will enable Asian Women to participate fully n the
social, cultural and civic life of the Borough.
It gives me great pleasure to present Naree Shakti’s
2015-16 Annual Report. I am pleased to announce
that we survived another year in this very difficult
financial climate. Our services are very popular and
we are
constantly getting new members to our
groups which highlights the demand for our
services. We received news that our funds from the
London Borough of Enfield will be reduced in the
coming financial year and therefore we have been
busy developing our contingency plan to keep our
very valuable service to Asian Women alive beyond
2016.
Our weekly activities are as popular as ever
amongst the local community and we have provided
a safe,
comfortable environment for them to
come and connect with one another and reduce
their social isolation.
The hours of staff have been reduced this year and
we have had to be very economical with our ex-
penditure to ensure we are able to financially
viable in the coming years.
Towards the end of this financial year we were
awarded a small grant to raise awareness of dementia
in the Asian community and signpost individuals from
these communities to relevant services available to
support them. We are delighted to be have given this
grant since we will be able to assist in the Prime
Ministers
Dementia challenge of making London
Dementia friendly.
The future looks uncertain for small organisations
such as ours but we are hopeful that we will be able
to survive through these difficult times and continue
to support Asian women and their families in Enfield.
I would like to thank the trustees of the organisation
who have supported me throughout this year and also
the staff and volunteers who put in some very hard
work to ensure that are services are delivered suc-
cessfully.
Vijey Rattan

We have been very fortunate to have con-
tinued our services very successfully
throughout this year. Our weekly yoga
sessions are very popular and on average
40 women are in attendance every ses-
sion. The women benefit from partici-
pating in regular exercise in a comfortable
environment in which they are able to
enhance their health and wellbeing.
Our weekly drop-in sessions have attract-
ed a total of 119 women this year. The
drop-in sessions are a space for women to
come and connect with each other, share
stories, socialise and reduce their isola-
tion. These sessions sometimes have guest
speakers which deliver information and
advice sessions in order to keep the wom-
en updated with services that are available
for them in the Borough.
Our Lloyds Foundation Trust funding has
enabled us to provide support and advice
to 48 women during this period. We have
dealt with welfare benefit enquires, hous-
ing issues, health and social care referral's
and domestic violence support as well as
many other areas of enquiries. This project
has been valuable in supporting vulnera-
ble Asian women in gaining support and
enabling them to make informed choices
in their lives.
Our monthly luncheon clubs are still very
popular and we attract on average 50
Asian women to these sessions. The hot
food and friendly atmosphere gives an
opportunity to take a break from the cook-
ing routine for many women, but also
gives them a sense of belonging and cre-
ates community cohesion.
Taking a break from our daily routines is
advised on a regular basis. Some of our
clients are not able to go on holiday or take a
short day trip due to the lack of support availa-
ble to them. During this financial period we
have provided 6 social events for women to
participate in and create memories for them-
selves. These event often provide a lifeline for
our clients since many of our clients are elderly
widowers who would not be able to travel
alone due to lack of confidence and language
barriers.
Our Friday Friendship group was introduced
this year and proved to be popular with some
of our women. The group would meet weekly
and participate in brain training exercises,
small group activities and creative sessions for
the women to engage in.
The emphasis on partnership working was just
as strong as previous years. Our Safeguarding
event which took place in March 2016 was
jointly organised with Enfield Council, Metro-
politan Police and Enfield Children and Young
Person’s Project. The speakers discussed issues
of domestic violence, female genital mutilation,
doorstep crime and safeguarding,. The event
was very successful and 78 people participated
on the day.
We would not have been able to provide these
services if we did not have the help and assis-
tance of our dedicated volunteers. We are
thankful to our 13 volunteers who on a weekly
basis ensure that our services are delivered
successfully.
