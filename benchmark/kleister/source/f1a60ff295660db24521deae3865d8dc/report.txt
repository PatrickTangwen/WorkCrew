GOVERNING DOCUMENTS
The name of the charity is Third Church of Christ, Scientist, London, England.
Third Church of Christ, Scientist, London, is a branch church of the First Church of Christ Scientist in
Boston, Massachusetts, USA, [The Mother Church] founded by Mary Baker Eddy, the discoverer and
founder of Christian Science. It is a lay church, firmly rooted in the teachings of Christ Jesus, and its
mission is to spread its message of Christian love and spiritual healing for the benefit and enrichment of all
mankind.
The Trustees have given consideration to the question of the church’s charitable status, with special
regard to the benefit to the community beyond its own membership. They have referred to the guidelines
on the Charity Commission website and are satisfied that both its activities and its use of funds fully justify
its charitable status.
ELECTION OF TRUSTEES/OTHER OFFICERS
In common with all Christian Science churches and societies, this is a lay church, governed by the
members. It has its own Constitution and Rules based on democratic principles.
The members elect from among their number, an executive board of three Trustees, one of whom is
President/Chairman, each serving for a maximum of three consecutive years, who receive no
remuneration.
While day to day management of the church’s affairs is in their hands, proposed expenditure exceeding
£1,500 at any one time is referred back to the members for ratification, except in cases of emergency.
Two lay readers, elected to serve for a maximum of three consecutive years, conduct the Sunday Morning
Service; one also conducts two W ednesday Meetings. Both Readers are paid appropriate salaries and
expenses, as agreed and regularly reviewed by the Executive Board.
The Sunday School Superintendent and teachers are appointed by the Executive Board from among
Church members and are unpaid. Other officers of the church (Treasurer, Clerk and Librarians) are
appointed by the executive board and are salaried. Duties carried out by appointed committees are
unpaid.
The soloist and organist are not church members, and receive appropriate fees.
TRUSTEES INDUCTION AND TRAINING
Trustees are elected annually by the members and may serve three consecutive years. Two years must
elapse before they may be re-elected.
Trustees must be members of the church for one year before being eligible for election. During this time
they are familiarised with the Rules and procedures of the church. The Manual of The Mother Church,
with which all members are familiar and use as a daily guide for thought and action, provides training
through its Articles.
All elected and appointed officials must be members of The Mother Church - The First Church of Christ
Scientist in Boston, Massachusetts, USA.

ORGANISATION
The organisation of the church is outlined in the Constitution and Rules. The Trustee Board oversees the
church activities with the knowledge and approval of the members. The members are empowered to elect
up to four Custodial Trustees who hold the property of the church in trust. These Custodial Trustees act
on the instructions of the Trustees, who, in turn, act at the direction of the members.
RISK MANAGEMENT
The Trustees review annually the major risks that could affect the church. These relate mainly to the
edifice itself, which is kept fully insured and maintained.
The Trustees consult annually with the Fund Manager of the church for the specific purpose of assessing
investment performance.
In the year 2000, the Trustees appointed a committee known as the FPPC (Financial Policy Planning
Committee) to monitor the church’s income and expenditure and make policy recommendations for the
Trustees' consideration. It was charged, in particular, with making recommendations regarding charitable
giving. The FPPC recommended that, in general, administrative and maintenance costs should be funded
from the income from ground rents, while income from the investment portfolio should be set aside for
charitable donations.
OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
The object of this church is to practise and spread the Truth as set forth in the Bible and Science and
Health with Key to the Scriptures by Mary Baker Eddy, the discoverer and founder of Christian Science.
An essential element of the religion is to teach and practice spiritual healing as recorded in the Bible and
demonstrated by Christ Jesus and his disciples and followers.
It is inherent in the tenets and practice of Christian Science that all its activities are aimed at embracing all
mankind. The public is warmly welcomed at all Church Services and lectures, and to use the reading
room.
Concurrently with the Sunday morning Church Service, a Sunday School welcomes children and young
people up to the age of 20.
Two meetings take place on Wednesdays, at which members of the congregation are invited to comment
on their experiences in the practice of Christian Science.
A free public reading room is maintained on the premises and is open every weekday, in which visitors are
welcome to enjoy its comfortable seating and browse an extensive reference library of Bibles and Christian
Science literature. These works include the Christian Science Monitor, a widely respected international
newspaper providing a weekly review of global news, as well as other weekly and monthly publications
available for study on the premises, on loan or for purchase. Experienced Christian Scientists are on hand
to assist and to answer questions from the public.
A free public lecture by a professional speaker is hosted at least once a year, explaining the principles of
Christian Science. In 2013 the church successfully held its Centenary celebrations, where members of the
public were invited to lectures and other activities in keeping with Christian Science.
THE CHURCH BUILDING:
The church was built in 1911 on its present site at 7 Curzon Street, London W1. The rear of the building
was later sold on long lease to a developer, while the facade and front of the building was preserved as the
church edifice.

THE CHURCH BUILDING: (Contd)
This is a listed building in the middle of London’s Mayfair, and is maintained to an appropriately high
standard, following recommendations for a rolling schedule of anticipated major repairs and maintenance
over a five-year period, by an architect appointed by the Trustees in 2006.
ACHIEVEMENTS AND PERFORMANCE
The work outlined under the Objectives and Activities has been carried out to a high standard by dedicated
lay members and substantial funds will be charitably disbursed.
The trustees of the Church are the members of the Executive Board. A major change in the past year has
been the vesting of the church’s real estate in the name of The Official Custodian for Charities. The
property was previously held by three church members who were the Custodian Trustees. The reason for
this change was that it avoided besting the property every time a new trustee was appointed. It also
makes the constitutional position clearer because there had been some confusion over the powers of the
former Custodian Trustees in relation to the Executive Board. The Church’s financial position has
improved as a result of the provisions of the new lease of the offices at the rear of the Church. One
benefit is that the lessees must pay a minimum rent of £100,000 a year; as opposed to the old lease which
stipulated no minimum rent. The rent will now be reviewed every 10 years (as opposed to the old lease
which had no reviews). Unfortunately, there were several months delay in the operation of the new lease
when the lessee’s proposal to renovate the courtyard was turned down by Westminster Council.
CHURCH INCOME
Income from offertories at Sunday Services, and annual per capita payments and donations from
members, form a modest proportion of the Church’s income.
No donations are made to individual members from Church funds: a separate collection is made at
members’ meetings to fund Care Committee disbursements, and payments in cases of need are made at
the discretion of the Care Committee. These are shown separately in the Accounts.
The church has two major sources of income both of which resulted from the sale of the lease mentioned
above. This produces an annual income from ground rents sufficient to cover all maintenance and running
costs, details of which are shown in the accompanying accounts.
The capital produced by the sale was placed with highly reputable fund managers, and the income from
investments is disbursed as donations.
CHARITABLE DISBURSEMENTS
In addition to the activities described above, the Church is in a sound enough financial position to be able
to make substantial donations to appropriate beneficiaries, by assigning all its investment income for that
purpose.
RESERVE POLICY AND FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT
The professional Fund Managers report regularly to the Trustees.
The FPPC, which includes the church’s Treasurer, keeps the income and disbursements under continual
review, advises on policy, and makes recommendations to the executive board for motions to be brought
to the members for additional donations, depending on the amount already budgeted and the balance
forecast by the Fund Managers as being available in any financial year.

In formulating this policy, the Trustees and church members agreed that the investment portfolio should be
maintained at a market value of around £3,000,000. The value at 31 December 2014 was £5,100,116.
On the same date, the projected investment income for the current financial year to December 2014 is
£250,000 which is sufficient to cover the regular donations and leaves a balance available for additional

PLANS FOR FUTURE CHARITABLE GIVING
Requests for financial assistance from appropriate organisations are first considered by the Trustees, then
referred to the FPPC for investigation. The FPPC then make recommendations to the Trustees, and if a
donation is suggested, it is brought as a Motion for discussion at the next Members’ Meeting.
Projected funds for the present year are adequate to meet present commitments, and a residual balance
is available for further giving as and when the need arises. Any residual investment income at the end of
the year will be re-invested for purposes of further charitable giving. The accounts’ records show that this
fund is carefully stewarded and the income is regularly disbursed for charitable purposes, so that only
reasonable amounts are carried forward from one year to the next.
The Church offers the public the opportunity to learn about Christian Science by attending services every
week, send their children to the Sunday School and use our Reading Room, which is open 4 hours a day 5
days a week. The Church also sponsors at least one free public lecture on Christian Science every year.

1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
a) Basis of Accounting
The Accounts have been prepared under the historical cost convention as modified by the
revaluation of fixed asset investments and in accordance with the Statement of Recommended
Practice: Accounting and Reporting by Charities issued in March 2005.
b) Cash Flow Statement
The charity has taken advantage of the exemption under FRS 1 not to prepare a cash flow
statement on the grounds that it qualifies as a small entity.
c) Incoming Resources
Investment income is taken into account when receivable and donations are taken into account
when received. Income from the rental of the Charity’s investment property is recognised on a
straight line basis over the period of the lease.
d) Resources Expended
Expenditure is included on an accruals basis.
•
Costs of generating funds comprise costs associated with the management of the Charity’s
investments.
•
Charitable expenditure comprises costs incurred in meeting the Charity’s activities. It includes
costs that can be allocated directly to such activities and those costs necessary to support
them.
•
Governance costs include costs associated with meeting constitutional and statutory
requirements of the Charity and include audit fees.
e) Tangible Fixed Assets
The freehold property occupied by the Charity for its own use is accounted for using the
revaluation method and is stated at its revalued amount less accumulated depreciation.
Depreciation is calculated on a straight line basis over 100 years based on the closing revalued
amount.
Other tangible fixed assets are depreciated as follows:
Computer equipment
33% straight line
Fixtures and fittings
15% reducing balance
f) Investment Assets
Investment assets (which includes listed investments and investment property) are stated at
market value. Gains on revaluation of investment property are credited to a separate designated
revaluation reserve.
g) Taxation
As the Church is a registered Charity the results of its normal activities are not liable for tax.
h) Stock
Stocks of reading material are stated at cost.
