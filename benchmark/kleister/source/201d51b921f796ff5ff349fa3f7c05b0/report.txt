Objectives and Activities
The charity's objects are to:
1 - Advance the Christian religion in any part of the world for the benefit of the public through
~
Holding prayer meetings, lectures and seminars
~
Producing and/or distributing literature and providing a place of retreat and worship to
enlighten others about the Chiistian religion and to assist people in their spiditual growth;
and
~
Such other means as the Trustees may determine
2 - Further the religious and other charitable work of Christian churches in the United Kingdom
3 —Promote any further charitable purpose for the benefit of the public as the Trustees may
determine
Activities for the Public Benefit
The Trustees confirm that they have complied with the duty in section 17(5) of the Charities Act
2011 to have due regard to the Charity Commission's general guidance on public benefit,
'Charities and Public Benefit'.
The main activities undertaken for the public benefit are as follows:
~
Facilitating and leading numerous courses, seminars and events held in Northumbria as
well as throughout UK, Europe and North America in furthering religious education.
~
Providing a variety of resources through our centre's activities (including its well-stocked
library), which help to raise awareness of our Christian beliefs and practices.
~
Encouraging and facilitating religious education and practice by providing and maintaining
places of worship, chapels, prayer rooms and houses for retreats and various courses.
~
Involvement in conferences, festivals, gatherings and consultations in UK, Europe,
Australia and North America to promote spiritual, mental and physical well-being.
~
Working ecumenically with churches of all denominations, theological colleges, and many
other diverse organisations in the private, public and voluntarylcommunity sectors to help
in addressing the spiritual, mental and social needs of the public.
~
Running retreats, workshops and pilgrimages to various Celtic Christian sites
~
Providing and promoting a Rule of Life as a moral and ethical framework for a way for
living that is capable of influencing society in a beneficial way.
~
Providing access to our facilities and activities for all who wish to enjoy them.

Achievements and Performance
The first part of the year was dominated by the issue of the debt to the Baptist Union pension
scheme (see Note 8 to the Accounts on page 15) so we are delighted to report that the debt was
met in full by its due date in the financial year without jeopardizing the work of the charity, and are
very thankful that this liability will not be passed on to future generations. We are deeply grateful
for the generosity of members and supporters, who contributed 8114,539 in additional giving.
Much of the focus of the year 2016-17 was on clearing this debt. However, alongside that, the
Community's leaders reflected deeply on the future needs of the charity, which led to the
emergence of some clear directions for our ongoing development. In the midst of the challenges,
it has been extremely heartening to see the wonderful and affirming response of our members
and supporters. This has, we believe, actually strengthened the charity and provided a stronger
base on which to build for the future, financially and as a community.
The growth in the number of people using our retreat facility, publications and resources, together
with an increase in membership and supporters demonstrates an appreciation and need for the
charity's work. Over the year the charity has:
~

~
Developed an interactive e-book version of its prayer book, Celtic Daily Prayer, which due for
publication autumn 2017
~
Hosted some 80 three/four day retreats at its centre in Northumberland
~
Hosted over 100 quiet days for individuals and leadership teams at its centre
~
Provided mentoring for leadership groups and individuals from the 'New Monastic' movement,
and for the traditional Christian denominations.
~
Led pilgrimages to sacred Christian sites
~
Contributed to development work within a number of Christian denominations through
workshops, consultations and seminars.
~
Contributed to theological training through Cranmer Hall and Spurgeon's College
~
Provided resources for various ecumenical Christian initiatives and conferences
~
Hosted various non-denominational groups and gatherings for people from across the
spectrum of Christian faith and the exploration of the Christian faith in the UK and across the
world.

Financial Review
The financial result for the year is a surplus on Unrestricted Funds of 8207,408 (2016: deficit
6111,680) before a transfer from Restricted Funds of E1,816 (2016: FNil).
The 2016 result includes an exceptional item in respect of the termination payment of 6172,450

As a result, the charity's Unrestricted Funds at 31 March 2017 are F226,405 (2016: E17,182).
The Trustees would like to acknowledge the support provided via both regular giving and
donations and via the significant use of volunteers, each of which enables work to continue and
the fulfilment of the charitable purpose.
Reserves Policy
As Trustees, we have reviewed the charity's needs for reserves in line with the guidance issued
by the Charity Commission.
The Trustees seek to retain sufficient Unrestricted Reserves, to cover all known liabilities and to
provide for three months' running costs to cover the charity's operational costs in the event of an
unforeseen reduction in income. This is estimated as a total requirement of approximately
F85,000. The statement of financial activities shows total unrestricted funds of 2226, 405 at 31
March 2017 (2016: 817,182).
The charity is increasing its investment in staffing and infrastructure costs by up to F50,000 in
2017-18. The Trustees are currently engaged in a review of longer-term investment needs
This policy is reviewed by the Trustees on an annual basis as part of the charity*s budgeting
processes.
Plans for Future Periods
We look forward to continuing to develop the activities of the charity over the next few years,
particularly around creativity for the production of digital and printed teaching materials, and the
retreat and event programmes at our retreat centre and throughout the world. This is in response
to an increase in demand for retreat and teaching materials, and also in response to a growing
interest in our work.
We will also continue to build on the work that we have already started around leadership and
governance, pdimarily in order to meet the demands of a growing organisation, and to enable
transition and succession planning for the future.
These developments will provide a stronger platform on which to continue building the charity into
the future.

1. Accounting Policies
Basis of Accounting
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention and in
accordance with applicable United Kingdom accounting standards, the Companies Act 2006 and
the provisions of FRS 102 Section 1A Small Entities. There were no material departures from that
standard.
Income
Income is included in the Statement of Financial Activities (SOFA) when there is entitlement,
certainty of receipt and the amount can be measured with sufficient reliability.
Such income is only deferred when the donor or funder has specified that the grant or donation
can only be used in future accounting periods or where the donor or funder has imposed
conditions which must be met before the charity has unconditional entitlement.
Income from Investments
Dividends are included on a receivable basis. Interest receivable on fixed interest securities and
bank deposits is included on an accruals basis.
Expenditure
All expenditure is accounted for on an accruals basis and is recognised when a liability is incurred.
~
Costs of Raising Funds are those costs of managing investments, seeking potential
funders and applying for funding.
~
Charitable Activities include expenditure associated with the provision of artistic projects
and residencies. This includes both the direct costs and support costs relating to these
activities.
~
Support or Indirect costs are those costs incurred in support of the charitable objectives.
These have been allocated to the resources expended on a consistent basis that fairly
reflects the true use of those resources within the organisation, such as allocating staff
costs by time spent and other costs by their usage.
~
Governance costs are those incurred in the governance of the charity and its assets and
are primarily associated with constitutional and statutory requirements.
Irrecoverable VAT
All resources expended are classified under activity headings that aggregate all costs related to
the category. Irrecoverable VAT is charged against the category of resources expended for which
it was incurred.

8.
Exceptional Item - Pension Scheme Settlement
The Noithumbria Community, prior to its conversion into a Charitable Incorporated Organisation
(CIO), was a participating employer in the Baptist Pension Scheme, a multi-employer, defined
benefit scheme.
It is believed that a cessation event took place on 30 June 2014 when the Community converted
into a CIO, without making use of the statutory easement available under the Occupational
Pension Scheme (Employer Debt and Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2010.
Under Section 75 of the Pensions Act 1995, an organisation that participates in a multi-
employer pension scheme triggers an employer debt payment if it ceases to have any active
members in the pension scheme when other employers continue to have active members, and
the pension scheme is in deficit.
The Baptist Pension Scheme was in deficit at 30 June 2014.
The employer debt owing by the Community at 30 June 2014 has been calculated as 2172,450.
This was provided in full in the year to 31 March 2016 and shown as an Exceptional Item of
expenditure in the Statement of Financial Activities and as an Exceptional Creditor (within Note
15).
The Community continued to pay enhanced employer contributions in respect of the pension
deficit between 30 June 2014 and 31 March 2016, amounting to 65,389, until it became aware
that a cessation event had taken place.
The debt of 65,389 referred to above was refunded in full to the Community in the year to 31
March 2017.
All liabilities arising from the settlement were paid in full in the year to 31 March 2017.
