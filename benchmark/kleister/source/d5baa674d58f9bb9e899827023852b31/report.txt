Voyage Background
Voyage traditionally works with young people from disadvantaged communities characterised by low
educational attainment, high crime rates, high unemployment and a long history of conflict and violence. We
are conscious of how certain labels/stereotypes can shape attitudes and outcomes and we are committed to
transforming these communities. Voyage is responsible for the design, development and delivery of three core
programs reflecting the following themes.
° Empower - Our accredited leadership programme Young Leaders for Safer Cities (YLfSC) has
been in operation for over 8 years. YLFSC has been successful at awarding over 1200 accredited
certificates to young people making outstanding commitments to their community.
° Transform - Our criminal justice work and 9 years’ experience delivering our innovative Horizons
(non-accredited) course has helped reduce offending behaviours and challenged reoffending lifestyles
for over 350 BAME young people under 18.
° Sustain - Our graduate Alumni programme now called the South Hackney Leadership Academy
(SHLA) is firmly established offering structured activities designed to support learning, progression
and work experience. The SHLA is a more focused level of working building on our young leaders
initiative were we commit time to a smaller cadre of young graduates who wish to take up distinct
positions in their community and within Voyage.
Our aims and objects
The charity’s purpose and aims as set out in the objects contained in the charity’s Article of Association are:
© to promote active and responsive citizenship particularly amongst members of the public who are
black or from other minority ethnic groups.
° to empower, advocate, facilitate support for and champion the needs and voice of young people
involved in the criminal justice system who are from the black and minority ethnic communities.
° to provide and promote education, community development and social inclusion of members of the
public who are black or from other minority ethnic groups.
o to reduce the disproportionate number of young offenders that are from black and minority ethnic
communities.
Voice of youth and genuine empowerment (V.O.Y.A.G.E) has now replaced the originally formed charitable
name “Black Police Association Charitable Trust (BPACT) reflecting its priorities for being youth led and an
independent ran youth organisation. The V.O.Y.A.G.E brand was adopted so that the complex relationships
between black and minority ethnic communities and the charity did not act as a barrier to delivering its primary
purposes.
VOYAGE's work operates under two related but separate programme strands guided by its themes and
impacts as follows:
Prevention and progression
VOYAGE’s preventative accredited course called Young Leaders for Safer Cities (YLFSC) is focused on
developing young leaders from challenging communities so that they can avoid making life changing choices/
errors. The YLfSC is supported by a graduate programme called the South Hackney Leadership Academy
(SHLA), which forms our progression and incubation development programme deepening our impacts. The
SHLA project is the central developmental vehicle helping to identify, support and capacity build a top table
team of highly motivated young leaders who will help us progress our youth take over plans in time for 2020.
Young participants will be supported by our plans to appoint a youth leadership coordinator who will ensure
young people:
Assist Voyage expansion into new locations
Support and underpin programme delivery and development
Take up significant leadership roles in their community and beyond
Influence, shape and consult on youth and community safety policies and strategies
Join Voyage’s emerging Youth advisory group and positions as Trustees

Reducing offending and challenging reoffending
The "Horizons Plus" in reach programme has replaced its outreach predecessor Horizons. The new
programme reflects Voyages partnership with Hackney Community College and has been redesigned to
include skills training to ensure young people with a criminal justice past can progress into mainstream
education and employment through raised confidence, a personalised developmental plan and on-going
support in the form of drop in mentoring. Voyage will encourage motivated young leaders from this programme
strand to support our youth take over plans. Horizons Plus is based on providing confidence and skills to young
people that have been part of the criminal justice system and/or at risk of being excluded/NEET.
Structure, Governance and Management
Voyage is a charitable delivery arm of the Met Black Police Association (MBPA) set up in 1998, and was
registered as a Charity in 2002 and the registration number is 1068108.
The charitable organisation is governed by the Articles of Association adopted on 7th October 2001. The
trustees are appointed or reappointed annually at an annual meeting of the Board of Trustees. Appointment
and retirement of trustees is done in accordance with its Articles of Association requiring appointments and
retirements should be by ordinary resolution. All trustees give their time voluntarily and receive no pecuniary
benefit from the charity.

Future plans
Medium Term
Over the next two years Voyage seeks to develop a two-tier strategy to deliver on important strategic
commitments and operational promises as set out in a new business plan underpinned by a comprehensive
marketing and communication strategy. These strategic documents will be produced and developed by the
senior management team and Trustees supported by a new organisational theory of change guiding the
Voyage towards a youth led organisation in time for 2020. An adoptive framework of institutional strengthening
will complement the strategic work focusing on the production of:
New constitutional and governance documentation
Data protection strategies and guidance
Intellectual property protections
Risk management and safeguarding documentation
Voyage will twin track its institutional strengthening work through the appointment of a new Youth Leadership
Coordinator (YLC) shaping and driving the youth take over strategy and expanding youth engagement work.
The YLC will establish a strong profile for Voyage and use this to garner support from partners and help
increase Voyage profile and reputation. At an operational level the YLC will:
° Recruiting young people to implement Voyage's youth take over strategy
° Sourcing and supporting highly motivated young leaders who wish work at higher levels within Voyage
® Setting up and coordinating a youth advisory board as a development vehicle for higher level
participation
° Embedding new partners and content into Voyage's varied projects and programmes
° Working with partners to develop a wider progression and participation framework for motivated young
leaders.
° Supporting the implementation of Voyage's evaluation framework
° Supporting research work and involving young leaders as part of a broader capacity building
programme
° Participate in external community safety fora, working groups and networks to identify youth
engagement opportunities
The programme’s local impact will create significant additional funding and media interest whilst providing a
powerful framework to enable income generation opportunities.
Long Term
The long-term aim of Voyage is to have an influence and the actions of the police, community safety
practitioners and organisations that impact young people of BAME origin. The charity will do this by creating a
team of young people that are informed and committed to supporting Voyage and the areas in which it works
and ultimately having an extensive framework of peer to peer work and impactful involvement in the decision-
making process. The areas of impact that Voyage wants to see in the long term are:
o Police - Influencing and shaping policing and community safety procedures and shaping tactics and
operations.
° Policy and Research - Ensuring that young people are involved in the policy and pieces of legislation
that impact their work. Voyage’s young people have been involved in a number of pieces of work
including consulting contribution to The Lammy Review and Voyage will ensure that this work is
expanded.

Reserves policy
The Board has assessed the charity’s requirements for reserves in light of the main risks to the organisation.
As aresult, the trustees have established fundraising strategy which includes ensuring a better mix of restricted
and unrestricted funding. Once this has been achieved, a reserve policy will be put in place whereby the
unrestricted funds not committed should be held in reserve and maintained at a level which ensures that
VOYAGE'’s core activity can continue during a period of unforeseen difficulty. This represents at least three
months’ (13 weeks) expenditure of about £18,000.This will be reviewed annually.

ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Basis of preparing the financial statements
Going concern
VOYAGE has had to scale operations back after losing its main funder (MOPAC) in 2014. Since then
Voyage has managed to sustain itself through small project based fund raising activities, working in
partnership and securing commercial work via public sector commissions and tenders. VOYAGE is
now sufficiently reduced in scale with no liabilities, a small yet dedicated team, minimal overheads
and relies on a supportive network of volunteers, strategic/ operational partners and active
Trustees. Voyage Trustees are fully engaged in the hard work and efforts of the team who have been
able to manage a reduced program of youth engagement whilst submitting an increased number of
high quality applications to funders with greater success. Voyage has been able draw on increased
staff capacity to negotiate a contract for services with its delivery partner Hackney Community
College.
Trustees are assured sufficient efforts are being made to increase applications and contractual work
and are fully engaged in the work of external consultants The Social Investment Consultancy (TSIC)
assisting with business planning and devising a new organisational theory of change.
Therefore, given the continuing efforts to secure new funding and the fact that the charity is able to
control the majority of its costs, as it does not have significant fixed overheads and if necessary is able
to defer or cancel courses and projects, the Trustees continue to adopt the going concern basis of
accounting.
Accounting convention
The accounts have been prepared in accordance with Accounting and Reporting by Charities:
Statement of Recommended Practice applicable to charities preparing their accounts in accordance
with the Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Ireland (FRS 102) (effective 1 January
2015) and applicable regulations.
Incoming resources
All incoming resources are included on the Statement of Financial Activities when the charity is legally
entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with reasonable accuracy.
- Voluntary income is received by way of donations and gifts and is included in full in the year in which
they are receivable.
- Income from grants is recognised on a receivable basis.
Resources expended
Expenditure is accounted for on an accruals basis and has been classified under headings that
aggregate all costs related to the category. Where costs cannot be directly attributed to particular
headings they have been allocated to activities on a basis consistent with the use of resources.
Tangible fixed assets
Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off each asset over its
estimated useful life.
Plant and machinery - 20% on reducing balance
Fixtures and fittings - 20% on reducing balance
Computer equipment - 33% on reducing balance
Taxation
The charity is exempt from tax on its charitable activities.
